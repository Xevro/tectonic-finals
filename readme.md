# Mantel by KBC

Mantel is a proposed ecosystem in KBC Mobile for later life. It is the care of a parent, held in one place for the whole family.

KBC already does this for two long projects. MyHome is the house. MyMobility is the car. Later life is longer than either, and today it has no hub. About 27 products sit across pensions, hospital cover, the digital vault, inheritance, service vouchers and funeral cover. The family stitches them together alone.

The proposal is for the KBC Tectonic finals. It is a direction, not a finished product.

## The family

Sophie, 56, works full time. Her parents, Jos (84) and Annie (81), live 40 minutes away. One week looks like this: a fall, a long wait on the phone with the mutuality, a shoebox of unopened letters, then a hip, an ambulance, and a discharge on Friday.

One in four Belgians aged 55 to 64 cares for someone every week. Belgians over 80 rise from about 663,000 today to about 1.21 million by 2050.

Mantel gives that family one plan, one Care Captain, and one promise. The Care Captain is a nurse or social worker from a partner organisation, paid by Mantel, looking after about 150 families. Kate does the admin.

There are two ways in. The parent can start at 75, at retirement, or when a partner dies, because they want to stay in charge. The child can start when a parent needs help. The plan itself is free. Care coordination is €35 a month, paid by the parent, once care is needed.

## Who sees what

Jos owns the household. He pays from his own account. He decides who may see the care, who may see the money, who may approve a payment, who may add money, and who is an emergency contact. Every view and payment is logged. He can change or remove a role in one tap.

Payments under a limit he sets are paid on their own. In the story, that limit is €500. Above it, a person he names has to agree. In the story, that person is Sophie.

| Person | Place in the plan |
| --- | --- |
| Jos, 84 | Owner and payer. Sets the rules. |
| Annie, 81 | Partner at home. One number, the Care Captain. The bills pay themselves. |
| Sophie, 56 | Daughter. Approves what is over the limit. Under it, she sees one line: Handled. |
| Pieter, 53 | Son, abroad. Sees the plan and can add €300 a month. Adding money is separate from approving it. |
| Lotte, 24 | Granddaughter. Emergency contact. She can receive a gift from Jos, his way. She does not see the pot or the bills. |

On the night of the discharge, a Care Captain named Ines gets the house ready for Friday at 16:00: a bed in the living room, a nurse twice a day, a meal at noon, and the hospital bill claimed through Hospital Care. The bed is over the limit, so it waits for Sophie. If the house is not ready, KBC pays a short-stay bed.

Jos also chooses how proactive KBC may be: silent, tell him, suggest, prepare, or act after his OK.

## Who takes part

Three parties share one ecosystem.

The family gives consent, the rules, and the €35 a month from the parent's account. In return they get one person to call, one plan, and a view of how long the money lasts.

KBC runs Mantel the way it runs MyHome. It brings Kate, the payments, the discharge guarantee, and one team. Deposits and care payments stay at KBC. KBC earns the coordination fee and a booking fee on care that partners deliver.

Partners do the care. Home nursing, family help, care homes, mutualities, notaries, funeral directors, alarms and daily help already exist. Mantel is the channel that books them, pays them when the invoice matches, and keeps the parent's wishes in one file. Named candidates, such as Wit-Gele Kruis, Familiehulp and the mutualities, are public examples. None has been contacted.

## What KBC already has, and what is new

Most of the shelf is already for sale: Hospital Care, Guardian Angel, KBC-Zorgplan, the digital vault, Helena, funeral cover, inheritance planning, a power of attorney, and Save for someone else.

Five pieces are proposed:

1. Care coordination, with a named Care Captain and Kate.
2. A family care pot: the parent's own account, with roles, limits and one shared view.
3. A discharge guarantee: a short-stay bed if the house is not ready.
4. A care runway: how long the money lasts at home or in a care home.
5. A care bridge: short credit, secured on the house, while it is being sold.

The same journey is how KBC already sells the next product. A mortgage client usually takes the home insurance and the life insurance with it. Mantel is that pattern applied to the longest journey a family has, including Sophie's own cover once she has seen what care costs, and Lotte's first savings.

## The prototype

A clickable prototype sits next to this proposal. Open `index.html` in the `tectonic-finals` folder.

The chips above the phone are the five people. Keys 1 to 5 switch person. Start shows MyHome, MyMobility and Mantel in one app. Inside Mantel there are three screens: Plan, Circle and Tonight. Circle is Jos's controls. Tonight is the discharge, and Sophie's one approval.

The prototype is a high-level demo for a video or a conversation. It leaves out the fee model, the partner list and the pilot maths.

## What comes next

A six-month pilot with 1,000 families in Mechelen and Leuven. The working target beyond that is 50,000 paying families, about one in eight of KBC's clients aged 75 and over. MyHome has about 60,000 clients today.

The prices, the booking fee, the size of the care pot and the number of families per Care Captain are assumptions to test in that pilot.
