# Mantel by KBC

Mantel is the longevity ecosystem. KBC helps families buy a home and drive a car. The longest project a family runs is caring for ageing parents, and there they are on their own.

In Belgium, 1 in 4 people aged 55 to 64 cares for someone every week, usually next to a full-time job. They deal with ten organisations: the hospital, the GP, the mutuality, home nursing, the care budget, the care home, the notary and the bank. Nobody runs the whole thing. The number of Belgians over 80 almost doubles by 2050, from about 663,000 today to about 1.21 million.

KBC has hubs for housing and mobility. MyHome is the house. MyMobility is the car. For later life it sells about 27 separate products, across pensions, hospital cover, the digital vault, inheritance, service vouchers and funeral cover, with no hub.

Mantel connects those products into one service. Kate does the paperwork. A named Care Captain, a nurse or social worker from a care partner, owns the plan. Partners give the care. KBC pays, insures and steps in: if the house is not ready when the hospital sends a parent home, KBC pays for a short-stay bed.

A family can start in two ways. The parent who wants to stay in charge, at 75, at retirement, or when a partner dies. Or the child who says “Mum needs help”. KBC acts on moments it already sees, never on health data. The parent decides who in the family may see and do what, and how much KBC may do on its own.

The proposal is for the KBC Tectonic finals. It is a direction, not a finished product.

## Everyone gains

Families get one person to call and their entitlements claimed. They also get one plan, and a view of how long the money lasts.

Partners get clients who arrive prepared and are paid automatically. Home nursing, family help, care homes, mutualities, notaries, funeral directors, alarms and daily help already exist. Mantel is the channel that books them, pays them when the invoice matches, and keeps the parent's wishes in one file. Named candidates, such as Wit-Gele Kruis, Familiehulp and the mutualities, are public examples. None has been contacted.

KBC keeps the whole family as clients, across three generations. It runs Mantel the way it runs MyHome. It brings Kate, the payments, the discharge guarantee, and one team. Deposits and care payments stay at KBC.

The same journey is how KBC already sells the next product. A mortgage client usually takes the home insurance and the life insurance with it. Mantel is that pattern applied to the longest journey a family has.

## Price

The family space is free. A Care Captain costs the parent €35 a month. A crisis is a one-off €250–500. That is in line with what Edward Jones, Dutch care brokers and Dutch insurers already do.

A 6-month pilot with 1,000 families tests the price and measures the results. The working location is Mechelen and Leuven. The working target beyond the pilot is 50,000 paying families, about one in eight of KBC's clients aged 75 and over. MyHome has about 60,000 clients today. The size of the care pot and the number of families per Care Captain are assumptions to test in that pilot.

## The family

Sophie, 56, works full time. Her parents, Jos (84) and Annie (81), live 40 minutes away. One week looks like this: a fall, a long wait on the phone with the mutuality, a shoebox of unopened letters, then a hip, an ambulance, and a discharge on Friday.

Mantel gives that family one plan, one Care Captain, and one promise. Kate does the paperwork. The Care Captain owns the plan.

## Who sees what

Jos owns the household. He pays from his own account. He decides who may see the care, who may see the money, who may approve a payment, who may add money, and who is an emergency contact. Every view and payment is logged. He can change or remove a role in one tap. He also decides how much KBC may do on its own.

Payments under a limit he sets are paid on their own. In the story, that limit is €500. Above it, a person he names has to agree. In the story, that person is Sophie.

| Person | Place in the plan |
| --- | --- |
| Jos, 84 | Owner and payer. Sets the rules. |
| Annie, 81 | Partner at home. One number, the Care Captain. The bills pay themselves. |
| Sophie, 56 | Daughter. Approves what is over the limit. Under it, she sees one line: Handled. |
| Pieter, 53 | Son, abroad. Sees the plan and can add €300 a month. Adding money is separate from approving it. |
| Lotte, 24 | Granddaughter. Emergency contact. She can receive a gift from Jos, his way. She does not see the pot or the bills. |

On the night of the discharge, a Care Captain named Ines gets the house ready for Friday at 16:00: a bed in the living room, a nurse twice a day, a meal at noon, and the hospital bill claimed through Hospital Care. The bed is over the limit, so it waits for Sophie. If the house is not ready, KBC pays a short-stay bed.

Jos also chooses how proactive KBC may be: silent, tell him, suggest, prepare, or act after his OK. KBC acts on moments it already sees, never on health data.

## What KBC already has, and what is new

Most of the shelf is already for sale: Hospital Care, Guardian Angel, KBC-Zorgplan, the digital vault, Helena, funeral cover, inheritance planning, a power of attorney, and Save for someone else.

Five pieces are proposed:

1. Care coordination, with a named Care Captain and Kate.
2. A family care pot: the parent's own account, with roles, limits and one shared view.
3. A discharge guarantee: a short-stay bed if the house is not ready.
4. A care runway: how long the money lasts at home or in a care home.
5. A care bridge: short credit, secured on the house, while it is being sold.

Mantel is also how the next generation meets KBC. Sophie sees what care costs, and can take her own cover. Lotte can start her first savings.

## The prototype

A clickable prototype sits next to this proposal. Open `index.html` in the `tectonic-finals` folder.

The chips above the phone are the five people. Keys 1 to 5 switch person. Start shows MyHome, MyMobility and Mantel in one app. Inside Mantel there are three screens: Plan, Circle and Tonight. Circle is Jos's controls. Tonight is the discharge, and Sophie's one approval.

The prototype is a high-level demo for a video or a conversation. It leaves out the fee model, the partner list and the pilot maths.
